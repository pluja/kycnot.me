This KYCnot.me October 2026 update is the biggest change to the site in a long time. Reviews were rebuilt from scratch, karma is gone, moderation got a lot of work, and services can now claim their own page. Here is what changed, and why.

## Reviews are now experiences

Star ratings said very little. A one-star review could mean a frozen account or a slow reply to an email, and both counted the same. Stars were also easy to game.

Now you share an **experience**: what you used the service for, whether it went well, and if something went wrong, what it was (KYC asked, funds held, no support…) and whether it got solved.

Above the reviews there is a short **summary** of recent experiences: mostly positive, mixed or mostly negative. Each person counts once, brand new accounts count half, and once enough reviews have checked proof, only those decide. The full rules are in the [review guidelines](/docs/guidelines#how-the-summary-is-calculated).

## Proof stays private

You can back a review with an order ID or a screenshot. Only you and the moderators see it. It is not shown to readers, not sent to AI and not shared with the service. A moderator checks it, usually on the service's own order lookup, and your review gets a badge with the result.

A service can dispute a negative review once. You then get a few days to add proof. If you don't answer, the review stays up but is marked **Unproven** and stops counting. This protects honest reviewers from being drowned out, and services from made-up stories. See [proof and disputes](/docs/guidelines#proof).

## Karma is gone

Karma points were easy to farm and said almost nothing about whether someone shares real experiences. They are replaced by **account trust**, built from facts: published comments, proof a moderator checked, approved service suggestions and the age of your account. Breaking the rules recently limits what you can post. Details on the [trust page](/docs/trust).

## Moderation

Every comment is checked before it goes live. An automatic check holds comments that look risky, never because of whether they are positive or negative. Harassment and made-up stories are always decided by a person.

When a comment is rejected, you see the reason. Some reasons, like off-topic or not enough detail, don't count against you. You can also withdraw your own comment at any time, and restore it later.

## Services can claim their page

People who run a listed service can now claim it themselves. Add a TXT record with your username to the service's website domain, press a button, and that's it. No waiting for support.

Claimed accounts get a team badge, and their replies on their own service are marked as coming from the team and published right away. We check the record every day, so the claim lasts only while the record does. Owner status, and access to private case files, still go through us. Services flagged as scams can't be claimed. [How to claim a service](/docs/trust#claiming-a-service).

## Badges for your site

The badges services can embed were redesigned. They are larger and easier to read, show the service's domain instead of its name, so a copycat site can't reuse them, and can optionally show what users say.

## Docs and funding

The old About page was split into [docs](/docs): review guidelines, how listings work, how scores work and how trust works. Long pages now have a table of contents.

The [About](/about#how-kycnotme-is-funded) page now says plainly how the site is funded: donations and public affiliate programs, nothing else. Services can't pay us for anything. We never took their money, and never will.

## Smaller things

- Events and incidents for a service now share one timeline.
- The swap comparison loads faster and stays out of the way when the swap provider is down.
- The site says "comments" everywhere, instead of mixing comments and posts.
- Several fixes make the site more stable under load.

Everything is in the [source code](https://tig.cx/pluja/kycnotme). If something looks wrong, [let us know](/contact).
